The short version

Kawali has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your saved recipes, your notes and your grocery list stay on your iPhone.

To turn a video into a recipe, the app sends the video's link to Kawali's server, which reads the public post and may use an AI service to help understand it. When you import a photo, the words are read on your iPhone and only the text is sent; the photo itself never leaves your device. Nothing that identifies you or your device is sent with an import, and we don't sell or share data for advertising.

Kawali Feast, the optional subscription, is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and RevenueCat, the service we use to keep track of subscriptions, holds the record of it under an anonymous identifier for your device, never under your name.

1. What stays on your device

Everything you keep in Kawali is stored on your iPhone or iPad, in the app's private storage:

  • Recipes: each recipe you save, with your edits, notes, favorites, pins and the part of the photo you chose to show, plus a copy of the recipe's picture (platforms' picture links expire, so the app keeps its own).
  • Groceries: your grocery list and what you have checked off.
  • Preferences: a few settings, such as how your recipes are sorted and whether English names are shown on the grocery list.
  • Reveals used: how many recipes Kawali has read for you this calendar month (see section 6). This one number is kept in your device's keychain rather than with the app's other data, so the free reveals don't start over when Kawali is deleted and reinstalled. It is a month and a count, it never leaves your device, and it isn't synced through iCloud.

This storage is shared only with Kawali's share extension on the same device, which uses it to hand a link or photos you share over to the app. None of it is uploaded to us. Deleting the app deletes all of it, apart from the reveal count in the keychain.

Backups: if you use iCloud Backup or back up your device to a computer, Kawali's data may be included like any other app's. Backups are controlled by you and covered by Apple's privacy policy.

2. Importing from a link

When you paste or share a link from YouTube, TikTok, Instagram or Facebook and tap Import Recipe, the app sends that link to Kawali's server. Nothing else about you or your device is sent with it.

The server then reads what the platform makes publicly available about that one post: its title, creator, description or caption, picture and, where available, captions, along with any recipe page the post links to. If that text doesn't already hold a written-out recipe, the server sends it to the AI service we use (see section 8) to turn it into ingredients and steps. The server answers with the recipe, which you review before saving on your device.

So the next person who imports the same post doesn't wait, the server keeps the finished recipe for that post in its recipe library, for as long as Kawali offers it. It is stored by the post, not by who asked, and it contains only what was read from the public post, never your edits. We may correct a recipe in the library by hand when Kawali read something wrong, and we count how many times each post has been asked for, never by whom.

When you share a link to Kawali, the share sheet also asks the server for the post's title, creator and picture, so it can show you what you're about to import. That lookup is not stored.

3. When Kawali watches a video

Some videos give no written recipe and no narration Kawali can read at once. For those, Kawali's service reads the public video itself, with help from the AI service in section 8, and works out the ingredients and steps from what the cook says and does. This takes a few minutes: the app shows the recipe as it stands, marks it as still being watched, and fills the steps in when they are ready (and can notify you, section 5). What is read is the public video, never anything about you or your device.

The server keeps a record of the job for as long as it keeps the recipe (section 2): the post's link, the public text read from it, what was read from the video, and the steps that came out of it, so the recipe can be read again when Kawali improves. It is about the video, not about you, and is not linked to any person or device.

4. Importing from a photo or pasted text

  • Photos: when you use Import Photo, Snap Photo, or share photos to Kawali, the words in the photo are read on your device by Apple's text recognition. The photo is never uploaded. Only the text that was read is sent to Kawali's server, so it can be laid out as a recipe.
  • Pasted text: when you tap Paste Text, the app reads your clipboard (iOS asks for your permission) and sends the copied text to Kawali's server.

The server reads the text and, when it isn't already laid out under headings like "Ingredients" and "Procedure", sends it to the AI service we use to turn it into a recipe. The text is used only to answer that one request, and the server doesn't store it afterwards (the AI service keeps it for up to 30 days, see section 8). Please don't include anything in a photo or pasted text that you wouldn't want processed this way.

5. Notifications

Notifications are optional. The first time you save a recipe Kawali is still watching, iOS asks whether Kawali may send you notifications. If you allow them, the app sends the server your device's push token, the recipe's title and an identifier the app created for that recipe, so it can tell you when the steps are ready. The server keeps these only until that video is finished (at most three days) and then deletes them. Notifications are delivered by Apple's Push Notification service. You can turn them off at any time in Settings › Notifications › Kawali.

6. Kawali Feast, the subscription

Kawali is free to use. Kawali Feast is an optional subscription that lifts the monthly limit on the recipes Kawali reads for you (we call each one a reveal): 5 a month without it, lots more with it. Recipes you write yourself are always free, and how many reveals you have used is counted on your device (section 1), never sent anywhere.

Feast is bought through the App Store, so Apple handles the payment under your Apple Account. Kawali never sees your name, your Apple Account or your payment details, and neither does anyone we work with.

To know whether Feast is active on your device, Kawali uses RevenueCat, a service that keeps track of app subscriptions. Each time the app opens, whether or not you ever subscribe, it asks RevenueCat about this device, and after a purchase it passes RevenueCat the App Store's record of it. What RevenueCat receives:

  • Two identifiers for the device, neither of them about you: a random anonymous identifier the app makes for itself (deleting the app discards it, and a reinstall makes a new one), and Apple's vendor identifier, an identifier Apple gives our apps on your device and resets when they are all removed. It is not the advertising identifier, which Kawali never uses.
  • Your Kawali purchases, as the App Store records them: the plan, when it was bought, renews and ends, the price and currency, and Apple's transaction numbers. Never your name or payment details.
  • About the device: which country's App Store you use, your device's language, its model and iOS version, the Kawali version and, as with any server it talks to, its network address.
  • How the Feast page is used: that it was shown, closed, or led to a purchase, and, if you use its subscription management page, the option you chose there (for instance, a reason you gave when cancelling).

RevenueCat uses this only to tell Kawali whether Feast is active and to show us, in its dashboard, how many anonymous devices subscribe and whether the Feast page works. It does not link the record to a person, and neither do we; it does not use advertising identifiers; and we have turned on none of its connections to other services. RevenueCat acts on our behalf under its data processing agreement and keeps the record on servers in the United States for as long as Kawali uses its service. You can ask us to have a device's record deleted (section 13).

Apple, for its part, tells RevenueCat when your subscription renews, changes, is cancelled or is refunded. Changing plans, cancelling and asking for a refund all happen on Apple's own screens, which the Feast page opens for you, and are covered by Apple's privacy policy.

7. Pictures, videos and links you open

To show a recipe's picture, the app downloads it from where the platform hosts it (for example, YouTube's image servers). Like visiting any web page, that platform receives your device's network address when the picture loads. When you tap to view the original video, open a linked recipe page or share a recipe, the destination you choose receives it, and its own privacy practices apply.

9. Server logs

Like most online services, our server and our hosting provider keep technical logs of the requests they handle, such as the time, the address requested (which can include the link you imported) and network information like IP addresses. We use them only to keep Kawali running, fix problems and protect it from abuse. They are kept for a limited time and are never used to build a profile of you or to connect imports to a person.

11. Information we do not collect

The app contains no advertising, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no third-party tracking code, and it does not use advertising identifiers. The one record of how the app is used is the Feast page's, described in section 6, and it is kept under an anonymous identifier. We don't know which recipes you save, what's on your grocery list, or who imported a particular link.

Apple may share anonymised crash logs and usage statistics with developers, but only if you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's Settings. That data is collected by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

12. Children's privacy

Kawali is a cooking app for adults and is not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age where you live). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

13. Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the California Consumer Privacy Act give you rights to access, correct, delete or export personal data a company holds about you, and to object to how it is used. Kawali is built so that we hold almost nothing about you: your recipes and groceries are on your device, where you can change or delete them, and what reaches our server is not linked to you.

The one record about your device that lasts is the Kawali Feast record RevenueCat holds (section 6). It carries no name, so we can't find it by yours: if you would like it deleted, email us and we'll work out with you which anonymous identifier belongs to your device, then ask RevenueCat to delete it. Your App Store purchase history itself is Apple's, under your Apple Account.

If you have any other question or request, email us and we'll help; we don't sell personal information, and we never will.

14. Security

Everything the app sends to Kawali's server travels over encrypted HTTPS connections, and only the app can use the import service. Payments for Kawali Feast are handled entirely by Apple, so no card or bank details ever pass through Kawali or RevenueCat. Your saved recipes are protected by your device's own security: your passcode, Face ID or Touch ID, and iOS encryption. Access to our server, our database and our RevenueCat dashboard is limited to the people who run Kawali.

15. Changes to this policy

If Kawali changes in a way that affects your privacy, we will update this page and the effective date at the top before the change reaches the app.
